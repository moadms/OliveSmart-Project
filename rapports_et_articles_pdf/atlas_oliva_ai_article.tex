\begin{abstract}
Accurate individual olive tree (\textit{Olea europaea} L.) inventory and canopy health monitoring from aerial and satellite imagery remain challenging due to small crown footprints, touching canopies, inter-sensor illumination shifts, and soil-shadow false positives. In this paper, we present a two-stage computer vision pipeline combining \textbf{YOLO11s} for high-recall crown detection with \textbf{YOLO11s-cls} for fine-grained canopy vigor classification into \textit{Healthy} (\texttt{sain}), \textit{Water Stress} (\texttt{stress}), and \textit{Suspect} (\texttt{suspect}). To preserve small tree crowns across wide orthomosaics, we introduce a Slicing Aided Hyper Inference (SAHI) $416\times416$ tiling strategy with 25\% overlap applied strictly after a 70\%/15\%/15\% train/validation/test split to eliminate data leakage. We further couple distance-transform apex centroid snapping with Gray-World color normalization and biophysical vegetation indices ($ExG$, $GCC$, $VARI$) calibrated against empirical Moroccan drought-stress benchmarks. Evaluated on an unseen 15\% test split of 21 images (2,297 olive tree crowns), the Stage~1 detector achieves a Precision of 94.72\%, a Recall of 82.75\%, an F1-score of 88.33\%, and an $mAP_{50}$ of 85.19\%, while the Stage~2 health classifier achieves 89.96\% Top-1 accuracy across 2,122 cropped crowns. Post-processing ablation on dense Haouz orchards eliminates stacked duplicate boxes and bare-soil false positives while achieving 0\% false positives on bare-soil control parcels.

\keywords{Olive Tree Detection \and YOLO11 \and SAHI Tiled Inference \and Centroid Localization \and Water Stress Classification \and Precision Agriculture.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Monitoring olive groves (\textit{Olea europaea} L.) across semi-arid Mediterranean regions requires reliable automated counting, precise trunk/apex localization, and early detection of water deficit and pathological decline. However, applying standard object detectors directly to very high-resolution (VHR) Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) orthomosaics or Zoom~19 satellite tiles introduces three critical failure modes: (i)~small canopies ($12$--$25\text{ px}$ wide) vanish during global image downscaling; (ii)~overlapping tile predictions and dense touching crowns generate stacked duplicate detections on a single tree; and (iii)~direct RGB thresholding misclassifies shaded foliage or dark bare soil as diseased (\textit{suspect}) trees.

To address these limitations, we decouple spatial localization from physiological health diagnosis in a two-stage deep learning framework:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage~1 (Spatial Detection \& Apex Localization):} A \textbf{YOLO11s} detector trained on SAHI $416\times416$ overlapping tiles with C2PSA spatial attention, refined via inter-tile Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS), Euclidean distance-transform apex snapping, and chlorophyll-guarded deduplication.
    \item \textbf{Stage~2 (Canopy Health Classification):} A dedicated \textbf{YOLO11s-cls} classifier operating on Gray-World normalized $128\times128$ crown crops, trained with strict train-only class rebalancing and calibrated against Moroccan water-stress and remote-sensing health benchmarks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Datasets and Preprocessing}
\subsection{Multi-Scale Aerial and Satellite Benchmarks}
We construct a curated multi-scale benchmark combining UAV and satellite imagery:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hybrid Olive Benchmark:} 140 annotated images totaling \textbf{10,931 olive tree crowns}, comprising 115 UAV/aerial images (9,669 trees) and 25 Zoom~19 satellite tiles ($0.25\text{ m/px}$, 1,262 trees) from the Haouz plain, Marrakech.
    \item \textbf{OAM-TCD Enrichment:} 30 high-resolution $2048\times2048$ tiles ($0.10\text{ m/px}$) streamed from OpenAerialMap Tree Cover Maps (\texttt{restor/tcd}) filtering individual tree crowns (\texttt{category\_id = 1}) to strengthen satellite generalization.
    \item \textbf{Health Calibration References:} Physiological drought-stress thresholds are grounded in Moroccan olive cultivar measurements (Mendeley Data \texttt{10.17632/22j26tpk63/2}) and multimodal UAV/satellite health monitoring (Zenodo OLEA-PRED \texttt{10.5281/zenodo.10920137}, ICAERUS \texttt{10.5281/zenodo.13121962}, and OliveTreeCrownsDb \texttt{10.17632/xym8rd2srf.3}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Leakage-Free Split and SAHI Tiling}
Prior to any tiling or augmentation, source images are partitioned into \textbf{70\% Training}, \textbf{15\% Validation}, and \textbf{15\% Test} (\texttt{seed=42}). Each split is then independently sliced into $416\times416$ SAHI tiles with a stride of $312\text{ px}$ (25\% overlap), yielding 460 UAV tiles (320 Train / 68 Val / 72 Test) and 25 local satellite tiles (17 Train / 3 Val / 5 Test) prior to OAM-TCD expansion.

\subsection{Gray-World Normalization and Vegetation Indices}
To remove sensor exposure bias between darker UAV orthophotos and brighter satellite imagery, each mosaic undergoes Gray-World channel balancing before computing Excess Green ($ExG$), Green Chromatic Coordinate ($GCC$), and Visible Atmospherically Resistant Index ($VARI$):
\begin{equation}
ExG = 2G - R - B, \quad GCC = \frac{G}{R + G + B}, \quad VARI = \frac{G - R}{G + R - B}
\end{equation}
For Stage~2 training, minority classes (\texttt{stress}, \texttt{suspect}) are augmented via orthogonal rotations and flips strictly inside the Training split, leaving Validation and Test splits 100\% real and unaugmented.

\section{Proposed Two-Stage Methodology}
\subsection{Stage 1: YOLO11s Crown Detection and Apex Snapping}
We adopt \textbf{YOLO11s} (9,413,187 parameters, 21.4 GFLOPs), which integrates C3k2 blocks and Pointwise Spatial Attention (C2PSA) to separate adjacent olive crowns. Training uses \texttt{AdamW} ($\text{lr}_0=0.001$), Cosine annealing, mixed precision (\texttt{amp=True}), and \texttt{close\_mosaic=15}. During inference, $416\times416$ tiles ($\text{conf}=0.18$, $\text{IoU}=0.45$) undergo:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inter-Tile NMS:} Suppresses duplicate boxes across tile borders ($\text{IoU}>0.25$ or center distance $<14\text{ px}$).
    \item \textbf{Distance-Transform Recovery:} Extracts local maxima ($\text{dist\_smooth} \ge 2.9\text{ px}$) on a denoised foliage mask to recover medium crowns missed by raw proposals.
    \item \textbf{Foliage Quality \& Apex Snapping:} Requires $\ge48\text{ px}^2$ of foliage ($\ge22\%$ box fill) and snaps each box center to the local distance-transform maximum, followed by an apex chlorophyll guard ($\text{ExG}_{\text{apex}} \ge 1.5$) and final deduplication ($\ge13.0\text{ px}$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: YOLO11s-cls Canopy Health Classification}
Each localized crown is cropped from the Gray-World balanced mosaic, resized to $128\times128$, and classified by \textbf{YOLO11s-cls} (5,437,971 parameters, 12.1 GFLOPs) trained for 50 epochs with ColorJitter (\texttt{hsv\_h=0.02}, \texttt{hsv\_s=0.7}, \texttt{hsv\_v=0.5}) and random erasing (\texttt{0.2}).

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a two-stage deep learning pipeline combining YOLO11s SAHI tiled detection, distance-transform apex snapping, and Gray-World normalized YOLO11s-cls health classification for olive groves. Evaluated on held-out aerial and satellite benchmarks, the framework achieves 94.72\% detection precision, 85.19\% $mAP_{50}$, and 89.96\% canopy health classification accuracy while eliminating duplicate crown boxes and bare-soil false positives. Future work will incorporate multi-band thermal/NIR indices and field-verified agronomic ground truth across additional Moroccan olive regions.
